Headline

“Women without partners”: the BIA rejects the social group

In Matter of A-C-V-M-, 30 I&N Dec. 236 (BIA 2026), Interim Decision 4274, the Board holds that “Guatemalan women without partners” is not a particular social group because it lacks particularity and social distinction.

To win asylum based on “membership in a particular social group,” an applicant must show a group that meets the three requirements the BIA itself set in 2014 (Matter of M-E-V-G- and Matter of W-G-R-): a shared immutable characteristic, boundaries defined with precision (particularity), and recognition of the group as distinct by the society in question (social distinction). In its new precedent decision, the Board applies that test to a group defined only by gender, nationality and the absence of a partner, and finds it fails the last two requirements: “without partners” draws no clear line between who is in and who is out, and it is not enough that Guatemalan society recognizes women in general — the applicant must show that society sees this subgroup as a category of its own. As a precedent, the decision binds immigration judges and asylum officers nationwide. In practice, gender-based asylum claims are not barred, but the group definition becomes the most vulnerable part of the case: it must be built on documentary evidence of how that society perceives the group, and should be paired, where appropriate, with alternative grounds — political opinion, for example — and a claim for protection under the Convention Against Torture, which requires no social group.

Official source: EOIR — Precedent Decisions, Volume 30 (Matter of A-C-V-M-, 30 I&N Dec. 236, ID 4274) ↗

Rules & Agencies

Proposed rule

Today is the last day: deference to prior approvals and notice to refugees

Two DHS comment periods close today, November 13. The first (FR 2026-20941) would strike from 8 CFR 214.1(c)(5) the rule requiring USCIS to defer to a prior approval when an H-1B, L-1, O-1 or other I-129 extension involves the same parties and the same facts; without it, every extension would be judged as a new filing. The second (FR 2026-20956) removes the 30-day notice given to a refugee before termination of status and cuts the response window to 10 days. Once comments close, DHS must review them before publishing any final rule — until then, the current rules remain in force.

Official source: Federal Register 2026-20941 ↗ · Federal Register 2026-20956 ↗
Final rule

Civil penalties for immigration violations: EOIR finalizes the rule and adds a mail-delay exception

The Justice Department finalized (FR 2026-21024, published October 14) the June 27, 2025 interim rule governing the imposition and collection of civil penalties for certain immigration-related violations. The only change from the interim text favors the noncitizen: a late-filed appeal may be accepted when a documented mail delay was beyond his or her control. For anyone who receives a penalty notice, the lesson is to keep mailing and tracking receipts — that evidence is what opens the exception.

Official source: Federal Register 2026-21024 — Imposition and Collection of Civil Penalties for Certain Immigration-Related Violations ↗
Final rule

EB-5: 17 days left to file at the old fees

The new EB-5 fees apply to petitions postmarked on or after November 30: Form I-526 goes from $3,675 to $7,615 (including the technology fee), the initial I-956 fee rises to $44,115, and the I-526E Integrity Fund contribution goes from $1,000 to $1,100. Investors with documentation ready save by mailing by November 29.

PERM & Labor Certification

Alert

PERM in about 10 months — and getting shorter

DOL data through October 31 show that PERM labor certification applications decided in the last 30 days took an average of 300 calendar days; over 90 days, 315; over 180 days, 360. The decline signals a faster-moving queue. Before PERM comes the prevailing wage determination (PWD): the National Prevailing Wage Center is now working on PERM requests received in August 2026 (OEWS-based) and July 2026 (non-OEWS); for H-1B, July 2026. Adding both stages, employers should plan for a little over a year from PWD request to certification. The math is decisive for H-1B workers: to extend beyond the sixth year based on a pending PERM, the labor certification must have been filed at least 365 days earlier — starting late can cost the status.

In Perspective

Guatemala, again: four decades of “social group”

The phrase “particular social group” has been in the refugee definition since 1951, but no one defined it in writing — and the BIA had to fill the gap. In Matter of Acosta (1985), the Board laid the foundation: a group is defined by a characteristic its members cannot change or should not be required to change. Almost three decades later, in Matter of A-R-C-G- (2014), the same Board recognized “married women in Guatemala who are unable to leave their relationship” as a group — a landmark win for survivors of domestic violence. In 2018 the Attorney General overruled that precedent in Matter of A-B-; in 2021 another Attorney General vacated A-B- and returned the issue to case-by-case adjudication. This week’s decision, once again involving Guatemalan women, shows the pendulum has not stopped: the door to gender-based asylum remains open, but each group is examined under a magnifying glass, and success depends less on the harm described than on how precisely the group is drawn and proven.
