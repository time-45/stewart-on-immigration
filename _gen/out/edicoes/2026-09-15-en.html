Federal judge blocks the end of “Duration of Status” on the eve of its effective date; the rule is stayed nationwide

On 09/14, the District of Massachusetts granted nationwide preliminary relief and postponed the effective date of the rule that would have replaced Duration of Status with fixed admission periods for F students, J exchange visitors, and I media representatives. The old framework stands.

On the eve of the day the rule would take effect — today, 09/15 — Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV of the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts, on 09/14, granted nationwide preliminary relief that postpones the effective date of the final rule and enjoins DHS and ICE from taking any further step to implement it while the litigation proceeds. In practice, the longstanding Duration of Status (D/S) system remains in force nationwide — students and exchange visitors continue to be admitted for the duration of their programs, not for fixed periods noted on the Form I-94.

The order answers the suit filed on 08/18 by a coalition of educational associations and labor unions — NAFSA, the Presidents’ Alliance, AICUM, AFT, GLO/AFT Local 6516, UAW and UAW Local 2322, and the NewsGuild-CWA — against the final rule published in the Federal Register on 07/17 (doc 2026-14439). Granting the preliminary injunction, the court found the plaintiffs likely to succeed on their claim that the rule is arbitrary and capricious in violation of the Administrative Procedure Act, and that letting it take effect would cause immediate and irreparable harm. The case proceeds as No. 1:26-cv-13799 (D. Mass.); plaintiffs are represented by McDermott Will & Schulte.

It is the turn this bulletin had been tracking since the 09/03 hearing: for eleven days the motion sat without a ruling, and the decision came on the very eve of the effective date. The government retains its appellate options and the case continues on the merits; we will monitor and report any development. Note, too, that today’s Federal Register carried no new acts from the agencies we track (USCIS, DOL/OFLC).

Official source: Memorandum and Order on the motion for a preliminary injunction, dated 09/14/2026 — U.S. District Court, D. Mass., No. 1:26-cv-13799 (case docket) ↗

Forms & Agencies

In effect

New editions of Forms I-539 and I-765 (ed. 09/15/26) take effect today, with no grace period

Regardless of the stay on the Duration of Status rule, USCIS keeps the requirement in place: as of today, 09/15, only the new editions of Form I-539 (Application to Extend/Change Nonimmigrant Status) and Form I-765 (Application for Employment Authorization), ed. 09/15/26, are accepted. There is no grace period — filings received on or after today using the prior editions (I-539 ed. 08/28/24 and I-765 ed. 08/21/25) will be rejected. The new editions were designed to align with the now-enjoined final rule; the USCIS alert, however, did not condition the forms’ mandatory use on the outcome of the litigation. Use only the 09/15/26 editions and watch for any superseding guidance from the agency.

Official source: USCIS — official alert on the new editions of Forms I-539 and I-765 (effective 09/15/2026, no grace period) ↗
